About Me

Hi! I'm Ethan, an astrophysics student at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill. My interests sit somewhere between physics, astronomy, computation, and engineering, particularly in problems where understanding the science is only part of the challenge and you also have to figure out how to measure, simulate, or build something useful.

I originally came to astrophysics through an interest in the theoretical side of physics, but over time I've become increasingly interested in instrumentation and experimental work. I've worked on astronomical research involving young stars and exoplanet hosts, radio observations and mapping at Green Bank, and numerical physics, while also spending more of my time designing and building things in robotics and mechanical systems.

What I enjoy most is the process of taking a problem that is initially vague or difficult and turning it into something concrete: a piece of code, an analysis, an experiment, a mechanism, or occasionally a very strange prototype. I'm especially interested in scientific instrumentation, robotics, observational astronomy, and engineering problems for environments where ordinary solutions stop working.

Outside of that work, I enjoy photography, astronomy, driving, and generally acquiring hobbies that give me another excuse to learn how something works.

You can find more of what I've worked on in Projects, or see my TEMP CV for a more conventional overview of my academic and technical experience.
